# PlushTrap Sales SERP Research — September 2026

Current results reinforce that balaclava shoppers describe the object visually: opening count, ears, color, pattern, material/style, and `ski mask` vs `balaclava`. Search architecture should preserve those attributes instead of relying on brand language.

## Query tiers

### Tier 1 — exact configuration
- bunny ear balaclava
- rabbit ear balaclava
- bunny ski mask
- 1 hole balaclava
- 3 hole balaclava
- 5 hole balaclava
- camo balaclava
- camouflage ski mask

Owner: exact PDP or exact collection only when that product/configuration currently exists.

### Tier 2 — color + product
- pink balaclava
- black balaclava
- white balaclava
- safety green balaclava
- pink ski mask
- black ski mask

Color URLs should be indexable only when they represent stable, useful product variants with distinct canonical content. Do not create thin pages for every filter combination.

### Tier 3 — style/aesthetic
- kawaii balaclava
- cute balaclava
- streetwear balaclava
- fashion balaclava
- cute ski mask
- streetwear ski mask

Owner: collection/editorial surface plus links to exact products. Use real product photography and descriptive alt text.

### Tier 4 — styling consideration
- balaclava outfit ideas
- how to style a balaclava
- bunny balaclava outfit
- camo balaclava streetwear

Owner: editorial/style content. These pages can convert well when the photographed product is directly shoppable.

## Cannibalization rule

A single exact product should own its configuration query. Collection pages own broad style/category queries. Editorial pages own styling/how-to queries. Do not make PDP, collection, and article all target `bunny ear balaclava` as their primary phrase.

## Snippet principle

Use noun + distinguishing attribute before drop/brand language. Example pattern:

`Bunny Ear Balaclava — [verified color/material/configuration] | PlushTrap`

Do not put unverified scarcity (`no restocks`, `limited`) or unsupported material/construction claims into titles/descriptions.
